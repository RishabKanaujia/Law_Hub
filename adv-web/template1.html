01 — The chambers

A practice built on preparation, not on publicity.

Diwalipura · Vadodara 390007
Adv. Devang H. Mehta

I enrolled with the Bar Council of Gujarat in 2008 and have since appeared in matters that begin as a family dispute, a property title question, a cheque that was dishonoured or an FIR — and end in settlement, acquittal, decree or appeal. Every brief is read personally, in full, before it is argued.

Instructions are accepted from clients in Vadodara, Ahmedabad, Anand, Bharuch and from non-resident Indians who need representation in Gujarat courts while living abroad. Where a matter is best served outside the courtroom, mediation and settlement are considered first — litigation is a strategy, not a habit.

03 — How a brief is handled

Four stages. No surprises.

From first call to final order
01

Consultation & honest merit assessment

Documents are read before the meeting. If the matter is weak, that is said in the first sitting — filing a case that should not be filed wastes a client's money and time.

02

Strategy, notice and documentation

Forum, limitation, relief and evidence are decided in writing. Notices, pleadings and applications are drafted in-house by the chambers.

03

Filing, appearances and argument

Admission, interim relief, evidence and final hearing are attended personally. Dates, orders and adjournments are tracked in a chamber diary.

04

Reporting and next step

After every effective hearing the client is informed of the order in plain language — Gujarati, Hindi or English — with the next date and the next step.
